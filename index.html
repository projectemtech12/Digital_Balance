WHAT IT MEANS

Digital well-being is about balance.

Digital well-being is the practice of using phones, computers, social media, games, and other digital tools in ways that support our physical, mental, social, and academic well-being. Technology can help us learn, communicate, create, and stay connected, but our habits matter. Responsible technology use means thinking about what we do online, how it affects us and others, and whether our digital choices support the life we want offline.

“

Technology should be a tool that supports your goals—not something that controls your attention.

“

DIGITAL WELL-BEING

Notice how technology affects your life.

◷

Time & Attention

Notice when scrolling, gaming, or notifications begin to take time away from schoolwork, rest, hobbies, family, or face-to-face interaction.

◌

Mental & Emotional Health

Pay attention to how online content makes you feel. Curate feeds, take breaks, and avoid letting online comparison determine your self-worth.

⌁

Physical Well-being

Balance screen activities with sleep, movement, meals, and time away from devices. Technology should not consistently interfere with healthy routines.

RESPONSIBLE USE

Think before you click.

01

Pause before sharing

Ask whether information is accurate, relevant, and appropriate before reposting it. Check reliable sources instead of immediately believing or spreading a claim.

02

Protect personal information

Think carefully about what you post. Avoid unnecessarily sharing private information, passwords, locations, or details that could put you or someone else at risk.

03

Respect other people

Communicate with the same respect online that you would show in person. Do not participate in harassment, cyberbullying, or the misuse of another person's work or information.

04

Give credit

Use your own words, cite information, and follow copyright rules when using images, articles, graphics, music, or other creative work.

HEALTHY HABITS

Small changes. Better balance.

01

Set intentional boundaries

Choose times and places where you will focus without unnecessary notifications or distractions.

02

Protect your sleep

Give yourself a screen-free wind-down period when possible so technology does not crowd out rest.

03

Curate your feed

Unfollow or mute content that repeatedly distracts, stresses, or encourages unhealthy comparison.

04

Make time for offline life

Balance digital activities with exercise, hobbies, family, friends, school, and time outdoors.

05

Check before you share

Verify important claims and consider the impact of your post before sending it to others.

The 3-question reset

  1. Why am I opening this? Identify your purpose.
  2. How is it affecting me? Notice your attention and mood.
  3. Is this helping my goal? Continue, change activity, or take a break.

REFLECTION

Your digital life is your responsibility.

Before your next scroll, post, search, or notification, pause and ask: Is this helping me become the person I want to be?

Use technology to learn, create, connect, and grow—while protecting your time, privacy, health, and relationships.
